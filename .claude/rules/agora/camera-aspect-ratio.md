## Agora Camera Aspect Ratio (the SDK default is 4:3)

**`AgoraRTC.createCameraVideoTrack()` publishes 640 × 480 — 4:3 — unless it is given an `encoderConfig`.** Agora's own API reference says it outright: *"The following table lists all the preset video profiles. The SDK uses `480p_1` by default."* Every camera in every Agora room was therefore publishing almost-square video into an interface whose tiles are all `aspect-video`, and the letterboxing looked like a phone setting rather than a default nobody had set. It is not the device: it reproduces on any camera, and it was found on a Pixel 9 Pro rear camera only because that is the one aimed at a stage.

* **The profile is chosen by role, in `AgoraLiveRoom`**, from `AGORA_CAMERA_ENCODER_HOST` (`720p_2` — 1280 × 720 @ 30) and `AGORA_CAMERA_ENCODER_PARTICIPANT` (`360p_4` — 640 × 360 @ 30) in `client/lib/constants.js`. The host's video is *the* video and can be watched full-screen; a meeting attendee is a small tile and `meeting` mode allows 17 simultaneous publishers, so 720p there multiplies uplink for pixels nobody sees.
* **Agora's preset table mixes 4:3, 1:1 and 16:9 with no visual cue** — `480p_2` and `720p_5` are 4:3, `360p_3` is square. Picking a preset by its name alone is how this returns. The 16:9 rungs are `180p_1`, `240p_4`, `360p_4`, `480p_9`, `720p_2` and `1080p_2`.
* **`setDevice` re-opens the device, so the profile is re-applied after every camera switch** (`useAgoraDevices.selectCamera`). The track object survives, but each camera exposes different capture modes and a phone's rear camera has native 4:3 ones — which is exactly where the square video was seen.
* **Whether the host may pick the quality at all is the admin's call, per event: `events.allow_host_video_quality`** (INTEGER, `NOT NULL DEFAULT 0`), a second checkbox beside «Consola móvil del host». Off, the event is pinned to 720p and no control renders anywhere. **Its visibility condition is deliberately wider than the console flag's**: it shows for any Agora live event, `broadcast` **and** `meeting`, because the selector lives in `AgoraHostControls`, which both modes share — gating it on `broadcast` alone would leave meetings, where there are more subscribers, with no spend control at all. **Turning it off must also override a stored preference**: `useHostVideoQuality` returns the default level whenever `enabled` is false rather than merely skipping the `localStorage` read, or a host who once picked 1080p on that phone would keep broadcasting 1080p in an event that forbids it. One gate does the hiding — `useHostMediaControls` returns `selectVideoQuality: null` — and both presentations already render the control only when the function exists.
* **The host picks the quality live, and the default is 720p for a billing reason, not a cautious one.** `AGORA_VIDEO_QUALITIES` offers 1080p / 720p / 480p, all three 16:9, applied on the published track with `setEncoderConfiguration` — no republish, no cut for the audience — and persisted per device. Agora bills **per subscriber, by the aggregate resolution that subscriber receives**, and the HD band ends at 921,600 px, which is exactly 1280 × 720: 1080p crosses into Full HD at **$8.99 per 1,000 min against $3.99**, so it is 2.25× the video cost of every attendee-minute and burns the free monthly bucket 2.25× faster. Add to that ~3–4 Mbps of sustained uplink and an hour of 1080p encoding on a phone. **The downward step is the one that earns its keep**: dropping to 480p from the console is what keeps a broadcast alive on bad venue wifi.
* **The camera track is created once and reused.** `setCameraEnabled(false)` calls `setEnabled(false)`; it does **not** close the track. So capture resolution and orientation are fixed for the session at first enable, and toggling the camera off and on will not renegotiate them — only a reload will. Consequence for the tripod: have the phone in landscape *before* turning the camera on the first time.
* **The host's own video is played with `mirror: false`; meeting tiles keep the default.** Agora mirrors the local video track and does not mirror remote ones, which is the right convention for a self-view on a front camera and the wrong one for the host: with the phone's rear camera on a tripod the host saw their own framing reversed — and any text in shot unreadable — while the audience received it correctly. `AgoraVideo` takes a `mirror` prop that is only passed on the three broadcast host-video elements; there it is a no-op for viewers (remote tracks are already unmirrored) and the fix for the host. A meeting attendee's self-view stays mirrored on purpose.
* Screen share is deliberately untouched: `createScreenVideoTrack` is a different default and no one has reported it.
